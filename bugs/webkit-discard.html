WebGPU in WebKit: a fragment shader with two discards and a handful of loops leaves NaN in the colour attachment

Every row draws the same 300 instanced blobs (96 triangles each) into a 512×512 rgba16float attachment cleared to black, with no depth attachment and no blending, for 60 frames, and reads the attachment back after each frame. The fragment shader writes one finite colour and discards some fragments, so every texel must be either the clear value or a finite colour. The rows differ only in the fragment shader, shown below. NaN texels are painted magenta in the image of the worst frame.

The failing shape: two discards, the second inside an if; then an if whose body holds about nine or more small for loops. Take away either discard, un-nest the second one, move the discards after the loops, drop the branch around the loops, or use fewer loops, and the attachment is clean. Chrome on the same GPU is clean in every row.

How it was found

Reduced from a full renderer (webgpu-aquarium) by swapping its 1,072-line fish shader for edited copies, one change at a time, against Safari 27.0 on an Apple M-series GPU (?only=fish&fishcut=both&seed=221315123&camera=reef&paused=1&time=0 in that app is the unreduced case). Around 15 NaN texels appear per frame at random positions inside the drawn objects; the count is stable, the positions are not. Everything the earlier standalone attempts tried, and that turned out not to matter: the hash used to decide the discard, texture sampling, derivatives, integer arithmetic, uniformity of the branch conditions, storage-buffer reads, and the amount of straight-line arithmetic (3,000 lines of sin/cos after the discards is clean). What does matter is the control flow: the number of loops after the nested discard.

Knobs, as URL parameters: ?frames=60, ?instances=300 (10 still fail; 1 and 4 are clean), ?scale=4 (instance size multiplier), ?w=512&h=512, ?mrt=1 (add a second, rg16float colour target, as the renderer has), ?depth=1 (add a reversed-Z depth32float attachment, as the renderer has), ?only=<substring of a row name>. None of the additions change the result.
